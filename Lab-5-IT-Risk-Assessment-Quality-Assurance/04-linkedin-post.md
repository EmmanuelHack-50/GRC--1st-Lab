# LinkedIn Post — Lab 5

**IT Risk Assessment Quality Assurance | Cybersecurity GRC Portfolio**

My fifth Cybersecurity GRC practical lab focuses on an important part of the risk management lifecycle that does not always get enough attention: **quality assurance of IT risk assessments**.

In this simulated EU technology-company scenario, I took the perspective of a GRC QA reviewer responsible for checking whether selected IT risk assessments were complete, evidence-based, consistent with internal requirements and clear enough to support business decisions.

I reviewed three fictional assessments covering:
- Privileged access and identity management
- Cloud backup resilience and recovery testing
- Supplier access and service desk oversight

The exercise involved checking assessment scope, risk statements, control effectiveness, evidence traceability, residual-risk rationale and remediation ownership.

I also documented QA findings, agreed corrective actions with the relevant assessor/service-owner roles, and identified recurring themes that could improve the wider assessment process.

**One key lesson:** A risk rating is only as reliable as the evidence and reasoning behind it. A control should not be described as effective simply because a policy exists or an activity is said to happen. The assessment needs traceable evidence, clear rationale and accountable ownership.

I have documented the review checklist, QA log, sample findings and continuous-improvement report in my GitHub portfolio.

This is a fictional, self-directed portfolio simulation—not a claim of conducting a real client assessment or audit. My aim is to keep building practical capability in IT risk assessment, GRC quality assurance, stakeholder engagement and continuous improvement.

I welcome constructive feedback and conversations with Cybersecurity GRC professionals, IT Risk Managers, Quality Assurance specialists and hiring teams across Europe and the wider international market.
