# Quality Improvement Report — IT Risk Assessment QA

**Reporting period:** Illustrative sample review  
**Prepared by:** GRC Quality Assurance (simulated role)  
**Audience:** GRC Manager, Risk Assessors and Service Owners  
**Status:** Portfolio exercise

## 1. Executive summary

A risk-based sample of three fictional IT risk assessments was reviewed against ten internal QA criteria. Two assessments were marked **Rework required** and one was marked **Pass with observations**. Three material findings were recorded.

The most visible theme was evidence traceability: assessment conclusions were stronger than the evidence references provided. The sample also highlighted the need to connect control effectiveness to residual risk rationale and to maintain clear service-owner accountability.

These results are illustrative and should not be used to infer the performance of a real GRC team.

The counts are based on overlapping themes; one finding may map to more than one QA criterion.

## 3. Recurring themes

### Theme 1 — Evidence is referenced but not sufficiently described
A statement such as "access is reviewed quarterly" is not enough by itself. A reviewer needs to understand what population was reviewed, when the review took place, who performed or approved it, what exceptions were identified and whether the evidence relates to the service under assessment.

**Improvement:** Add mandatory evidence metadata to the workflow: evidence owner, date, scope, source/reference, control supported and limitations.

### Theme 2 — Control effectiveness and residual risk are not always connected
An assessment may identify a control but fail to explain whether it is implemented, operating and sufficiently effective to reduce the risk.

**Improvement:** Add a short control-effectiveness rationale and require the assessor to explain how the evidence informs the residual-risk rating.

### Theme 3 — Ownership and follow-up need to be explicit
A finding without an accountable action owner and target date is difficult to track to closure.

**Improvement:** Make action owner, target date, status, response and closure evidence mandatory workflow fields.

## 4. Improvement plan

| Priority | Improvement | Owner | Suggested timing | Success indicator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce a standard evidence-reference template. | GRC Methodology Lead | 30 days | Sampled records contain date, scope, source and owner for key evidence. |
| 2 | Publish worked examples for inherent and residual risk rationale. | GRC QA Lead | 45 days | Assessors use consistent rationale in subsequent samples. |
| 3 | Add mandatory action owner and target date fields. | GRC Workflow Administrator | 30 days | No open QA action lacks an accountable owner or due date. |
| 4 | Run a short assessor calibration session using anonymised examples. | GRC QA Lead | 60 days | Reviewers and assessors demonstrate consistent interpretation of criteria. |
| 5 | Establish monthly QA trend reporting. | GRC Manager | Monthly | Trends, overdue actions and repeat findings are reviewed and minuted. |

Timings and owners are proposed for the fictional scenario and should be agreed in a real organization.

## 5. Suggested quality dashboard

Track these indicators over time:

- Percentage of sampled assessments passing QA first time.
- Number and severity of QA findings by criterion.
- Percentage of findings closed by agreed due date.
- Average time from QA finding to validated closure.
- Repeat findings by assessor, service or process stage (handled fairly and with context).
- Percentage of sampled assessments with traceable evidence references.
- Percentage of treatment actions with owner, due date and closure evidence.

Do not use first-pass pass rate in isolation. A high pass rate can reflect a weak sample or lenient review criteria. Pair outcome metrics with sample size, risk-based selection, reviewer calibration and finding severity.

## 6. Governance and escalation

- The assessor remains responsible for the accuracy and update of the assessment.
- The service owner supplies service context and relevant evidence.
- The risk owner remains accountable for the underlying risk and risk acceptance decision.
- QA records whether the assessment meets the required quality bar; QA does not silently accept risk on behalf of the business.
- Material unresolved gaps should be escalated through the agreed GRC governance route.

## 7. Conclusion

Quality assurance should make risk assessments more useful, not merely more polished. A consistent review process improves confidence that ratings are evidence-led, treatment actions are actionable and management reporting reflects the actual state of risk knowledge.

The next cycle should test whether the proposed improvements reduce repeat evidence and rationale findings. The QA criteria should also be reviewed periodically to ensure they remain proportionate and aligned with the organization's risk methodology.
